The Entity

An interactive altar-like installation I worked on as a part of a 6 person university group project. It aims to induce the feeling of sublime, as the participants face an entity that seemingly possesses more power than they do. The core interaction involved users finding a correct combination of artifacts by placing them on the altar and then pumping the "heart" of the cybernetic idol, thus reviving it. The installation was exhibited at GOGBOT2026 and invited to Dutch Design Week 2026.

I developed the main state machine in Chataigne that contained the main execution logic. This system also served as a centralized system for collecting, processing and routing signals collected from an ESP32 controlling artifact RFID scanners and initializing light sensor, as well as another ESP32 governing the pressure sensors and vibration feedback of the "heart".

Outputs of the system included the LED strips, visuals controlled by Touch Designer and a dynamic audio system. I implemented the audio system using Pure Data with sounds designed in Reaper and actuated through user inputs, routed from Chataigne through OSC commands. When recording sounds, along traditional microphones, I employed my electromagnetic microphone (Ether V2).

G-Systems

The playlist contains results of an experiment, where I attempted to use L-systems to modify an audio signal. The chosen sound manipulations are based on the theory of granular synthesis. Inspired by the ideas of emergence, my goal was to achieve a complex, unpredictable behaviour stemming from a simple set of rules. I was wandering how fractalous, self-repetitive nature of L-systems would translate to sound.

Resulting tool is a kind of a musical instrument that is very far from being intuitive, but an instrument neverhteless. It is deterministic, meaning that given an initial configuration (system parameters, L-system axiom and rules) the resulting sound can be reproduced. However "playing" it feels more like clueless tinkering, with rules looking like this:

F[dF++F++F+B]+F+F-B[d+tttFFF+F+FTT+FF-B-B-B-B]F++F++F++F

and amounting to 100000 symbols in the final "sentence" which needs to be parsed to sound. It is slow and convoluted, but can give some very interesting results, with output sounds ranging from drone to musique concrète. More technical description and source code is available on GitHub.

A Ruin

vishnya · aruin

A sound collage made for a university course. All source sounds were self-gathered. Here is the official description:

"This sound collage explores the feeling of panic, dissociation and pulls the listener into the chaos reigning in the vast expanse of a distressed consciousness. An overloaded conveyor belt of voices and thoughts being stuffed in one's head, each new one shoving the chain of previous ones deeper and deeper, inevitably leading to a collapse, or a rebirth.

The work does not include any coherent dialogue, instead it manipulates field recordings of various everyday objects and environments, allowing their unique, imperfect and unpredictable nature to come to light and guide the narrative. They are combined in a lush landscape with a sole purpose of stimulating the imagination, prompting the listener to envision their own story while steering away from comfort and mundanity."

Spectral expander

A small experiment in Python where an audio signal is transformed by uniformly scaling its frequency axis inside a STFT. It breaks the timbral patterns of a sound, acting like a broken pitch shifter. The tools also allows to shift the frequency data "up" and "down" the spectrum. Some interesting effects can be achieved if multiple instances of the processed signal are stacked together, which was the intent of the mixer-like GUI. Tbe example video demonstrates the effects on a simple sawtooch wave, but some weird sounds can be heards if you feed it voices or melodies as well. Code available on GitHub.

A celestial flower

Some visuals I made in Touch Designer. The repeating shape is a simple f(x,y)=x^2 + y^2 paraboloid, with its domain restricted to [-1,1] for x and y values. It looked like a tulip flower, so I decided to make a rose out of it. I instanced the shape with different scales and added rotation and some noise.
